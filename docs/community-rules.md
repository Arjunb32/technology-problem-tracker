# Community rules

Help people understand and solve technology problems with evidence and respectful discussion.

1. **Report a concrete issue.** Include the product, relevant device and software versions, symptoms, reproduction steps, and what you already tried. Search for a matching issue before posting.
2. **Confirm your own experience.** Mark a problem as affecting you only when it occurs on your setup. Use an upvote to express priority. Do not create extra accounts to inflate either count.
3. **Describe the scope of a fix.** Include instructions, supported setups, limitations, and risks such as data loss or a reset. Say when a fix is only a workaround.
4. **Test honestly.** Record the result for the revision you actually tried and explain your setup. “Not applicable” is appropriate when the instructions do not fit your device or version.
5. **Protect private information.** Remove passwords, access keys, personal addresses, account details, and confidential material from screenshots and descriptions. Share only material you are entitled to post.
6. **Treat people respectfully.** No harassment, hate, threats, impersonation, spam, deceptive links, or unrelated promotion. Discuss the technical evidence rather than attacking contributors.
7. **Avoid harmful instructions.** Do not post malware, credential theft instructions, destructive commands without a legitimate troubleshooting context, or instructions to exploit another person's systems.
8. **Use reporting and moderation.** Report content that breaks these rules. Moderators may request information, hide content, restrict accounts, merge duplicates, or change status with a recorded reason. A moderator badge or verified fix does not guarantee that a procedure is safe or compatible with every setup.

Before trying a change, read its warnings and preserve data you cannot afford to lose. Follow your device or software provider's guidance when it conflicts with a community suggestion.

The launch owner handles moderation during the beta. An appeal/contact route and response expectations must be published before public release.
